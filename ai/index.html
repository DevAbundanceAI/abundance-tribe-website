Why most AI spend disappears

A year of AI projects, and nothing moved.

This is the pattern in almost every business we get into. It is not a discipline problem. It is a sequencing problem.

What usually happens

Tools first

  • You bought the tools and your team logged in a few times, and three months later nobody can point at a single number that moved.
  • Somebody became the AI person on top of the job they already had, so none of it ever got past experiments.
  • You installed what worked for a business that looks nothing like yours, because that is what showed up in the feed that week.
  • Every vendor you talk to opens with their product instead of your numbers.
What actually moves a number

Constraint first

  • We start from your numbers, so the first thing you install is the thing that is actually capping you.
  • You get the five areas ranked for your business, plus how to install each one, so your team stops guessing and starts executing.
  • You fix one constraint at a time, which means you watch the number move before you spend anything on the next one.
  • We work above your team, not instead of it. Your people execute, and they own what gets built.

We call this Constraint-First AI Mapping, and it runs inside every engagement we take.

Before you book

The questions everyone asks first.

Are you an AI agency?
No. An agency wants to own a channel and keep billing you for it. We find the constraint that is capping growth, install the fix with your team, and leave them owning it. If the honest answer is that you do not need anything from us yet, you get told that on the call.
Does my team need to be technical?
No, and nobody on it needs to become the AI person. That role is one of the main reasons these projects stall. The install happens with the people you already have, doing the jobs they already do.
What actually happens on the discovery call?
Thirty minutes on your business. You leave with three things. Which of the five areas is most likely capping you. Whether your business is ready for AI at all, and if it is not, what has to go in first. And a straight answer on whether working together is worth it for you. You keep all three either way.
Our data is a mess. Is that a problem?
It is normal, and it is usually part of the finding. We would rather know early, because a constraint sitting in your reporting is far cheaper to fix than a year of decisions made on numbers nobody trusts.
How quickly does anything move?
You fix one constraint at a time and you watch that number before spending on the next one. That is deliberate, and it is also why we will not quote you a timeline before we have seen your numbers. Anyone who does is guessing.
What does it cost?
We talk about that on the call, once we both know what we are actually fixing. Putting a number on this page before that would be guessing, and you would have no way to tell whether it was worth it.
