ON-SITE Crane & Heavy Equipment Rentals

From a brochure site to a storefront that takes orders.

ON-SITE rents cranes and heavy equipment to contractors who need a machine on site tomorrow. The business worked. The website could only ask you to call. We built the storefront that captures the quote, uploads the insurance, signs the agreement, holds the deposit and books the call, then moved the whole domain onto it overnight with zero downtime.

THE SITUATION

Real machines. No way to order one.

The website was a WordPress brochure: browse a few photos, then pick up the phone. Every quote, every insurance certificate, every rental agreement and every deposit moved by phone and email, and none of it was recorded anywhere a business could learn from.

The owner did not need marketing. He needed infrastructure that transacts. Sending traffic to the old site would have been paying to generate phone calls.

WHAT WE BUILT

The storefront, and the plumbing under it.

Thirty-seven routes, no pricing anywhere on the public site, and a quote captured on the first click rather than the second.

  • Equipment listings with real availability, replacing the brochure
  • A two-field quote popup on every page, so a plain nav click on a machine page still records that machine
  • Insurance upload and verification built into the order flow
  • E-signature on the rental agreement, deposit held on card, and an owner approval step before any charge fires
  • A booking page with two calendars routed by channel, so a call booked from a Google ad is counted against Google in the CRM itself
  • Terms and privacy written for what Google Ads review and the SMS carrier registration actually check
  • A tracking phone number on every page and in the business schema, so calls attribute like everything else
THE CUTOVER

Live overnight. Nothing dropped.

Cut over the night of September 11, 2026. Backup taken, the full DNS zone inventoried, a duplicate mail record that had been silently violating the spec deleted, the new zone built with every record in place so the old site kept serving through the nameserver change, then apex and www flipped to the new site as the final instant step. Mail verified intact from outside after every step.

That is the standard for a domain move: the client finds out it happened because the site is different in the morning, not because their email stopped.

WHERE IT GOES

Vendor one of many.

The storefront and the MachineryMaps marketplace are the same codebase, architected multi-vendor from day one. ON-SITE launches as vendor number one and proves the model with its own fleet. The platform opens to other operators once the first one is transacting.

Next on this engagement is the Scale side: Google Ads onto a site that can now take the order.
